A fifth registry: Math Academy quizzes inside the correlation-game architecture

Asked whether bza could grow a Math Academy–style quiz feature, parameterised over a semantic tree of concepts the way the correlation games are parameterised over media. The answer is yes, and the mechanism is already in the codebase under a name that does not advertise it: lib/correlate/scope.ts, the fourth registry, exists precisely because "a medium is not a flat space." A concept tree is a scope ontology over the theorem medium. Two of the eleven existing games are already the right shape. But three of the turn engine's load-bearing guarantees invert under mechanical judgement, which is why this should be a sibling engine sharing the registries rather than a twelfth game sharing the route. Of the four requested surface features, three are largely already present in package.json; one is genuinely new.

What was asked

Whether bza could have a Math Academy–inspired quiz feature that, like the correlation games, is parameterised over a semantic tree of concepts — adding a notion of prerequisites, incorporating Math Academy's learning philosophy, and with support for Emacs-style keybindings, an RPN calculator, "math vibe coding," and a LaTeX pipeline.

This report answers the architecture question first, because it determines whether the four surface features are cheap or expensive. Everything below is read from the TempleDB-indexed bza revision and from mathacademy.com/pedagogy.

The mechanism already exists, and it is not the one you would guess

There are four registries in lib/correlate/, not three. The fourth is scope.ts, and its own docstring makes the argument better than a summary would:

Media say what an offering can be made of. Relations say what a reply can do to one. Tuning says in what proportion. None of them can say "music, but jazz" or "a stretch, but for legs" — and those are the restrictions people actually reach for, because a medium is not a flat space. Scope is that axis.

The implementation is two things per facet: a hint folded into the search and a pattern deciding whether what came back belongs. That is a generalisation of what offerings/knowledge.ts already does to carve four media (theorem, phenomenon, organism, place) out of one Wikipedia provider.

A mathematical concept tree is a scope ontology over the theorem medium. Not by analogy — by construction. "Algebra → rational expressions → partial fractions" is a region of a medium, expressed as a search hint plus a membership pattern, which is exactly what a facet is.

The honesty distinction transfers intact

Scope already declares, per facet, which kind of promise it can make:

Facet kindbza's definitionIn a mathematics ontology
checked "The provider gives us the field, or the text is authored here, so a reply outside the scope is detected and discarded exactly like a search that found nothing — the ordinary miss, which the retry stage already knows how to answer." A mathlib-backed concept. Declaration names, module paths, hypotheses and axioms are fields we actually have. Membership is decidable.
guided "The provider does not tell us, so the instruction goes in the prompt and the hint goes in the query, and that is the whole of it. The facet carries a caveat saying so, and the UI prints it." "The intuitive parts of algebra." No field decides it. Says so, visibly.

This is the same distinction as the Wikipedia-versus-mathlib fidelity gap raised in PROPOSAL-BZA.md §3, wearing a different hat. Music genre is bza's own honest example: Deezer returns no genre, so genre is guided and admits it, while music era is checked because the track re-fetch carries release_date. The pattern is established; mathematics just happens to be a domain where a very large fraction of the ontology can be checked.

Two existing games are already the right shape

Games are pure data — games.ts holds eleven, the turn route never branches on game id, and each carries a constraint string that states its one rule. Two of those constraints are, read slightly sideways, descriptions of a practice loop:

GameIts constraint, verbatimRead as practice
investigation "You choose where to look, never what you find." You choose which concept to probe; you do not choose which problem you get.
navigation "You pick the key, never the door." You offer what you know; it lands you at what that unlocks. A prerequisite walk.

navigation's world keys are here (where you are standing), atlas (places reached, each with one fixed detail) and route (order of arrival). Relabel those as current frontier, concepts reached with their strength, and order of introduction, and the structure is a prerequisite traversal with no change to its shape.

This is the idea worth building. Not a practice queue sitting beside a game, but a traversal of the concept space that generates the queue. It keeps the thing feeling like one product rather than a learning app stapled to a game, and it reuses a framing the codebase has already proven it can express as data.

Where the engine inverts — three places, and they decide the architecture

"Modes as data" is the property that makes an eleven-game space tractable, and it holds almost everywhere. It does not hold here, in three specific and non-negotiable places.

1. There is no mechanical judge

Judge = 'player' | 'narrator'. Both are interpretive: one is you ruling and the interpreter arguing, the other is an in-fiction interpreter whose ruling sticks. Mechanical judgement — a worker returning supported / counterexample_found / inconclusive — is a third kind, and it is a new ruling path rather than a data edit. This is the single place the data-driven design does not absorb the feature.

2. The reply guarantee is backwards for practice

The route guarantees a legal turn never persists without a real reply, through four stages: interpreter plan → corrective retry → server salvage search → composed floor. The floor reaches composed media through a mute because composed media cannot miss.

For a game this is humane and correct: a player who scoped themselves into a corner gets an answer rather than a dead chain. For practice it is dishonest. When the learner is wrong, the correct response is wrong, and here is the step that broke — not a salvaged offering that keeps the turn alive. The guarantee that makes the game kind would make the quiz lie, and there is no parameter value that fixes it, because the guarantee is the point.

3. Scope relaxation has the opposite priority

From scope.ts: checked scopes bite on the interpreter's plan and its one corrective retry, and are relaxed at salvage, "for the same reason a total mute is ignored by weightedReplyMedia: the engine guarantees that a legal turn carries a real reply, and a guarantee outranks a preference."

Prerequisite scope must be the one thing that never relaxes. Serving a problem whose prerequisites are unmet is precisely the failure mastery learning exists to prevent — it is the mechanism, not a quality-of-service degradation. Same machinery, inverted priority.

And a structural mismatch

Spaced repetition is cross-session and due-date driven. A turn graph is a branch within a session, with parent_turn_id and a world that evolves along it. Due dates do not belong in listen_turns, and a review assignment that draws from four concepts is not a branch of anything.

The fifth registry: concepts

Media / relations / tuning / scope answer what an offering is made of, what a reply does to one, in what proportion and from which region. None of them answers what depends on what. That is the fifth axis, and it is a graph rather than a flat enum, which is why it cannot be folded into scope. 

// lib/concepts/types.ts  (proposed)

export interface Concept {
  id: string
  name: string
  /** The scope facet that selects this concept's offerings. */
  scope: ScopeFacet
  /** 'checked' when a formal library backs it; 'guided' otherwise. Inherited
   *  from the facet, restated here because the UI needs it per concept. */
  fidelity: 'checked' | 'guided'
  objectives: Objective[]        // each names its task kinds; no kind, no objective
  knowledgePoints: KnowledgePoint[]
}

export interface KnowledgePoint {
  id: string
  conceptId: string
  statement: string             // one or two sentences, learner's language
  workedExample: WorkedExample  // REQUIRED. subgoal-labelled, not a flat list
  visualRef?: string            // dual coding
  position: number
  automaticityTarget?: number   // ms; absent means automaticity is not tracked
}

export type ConceptEdgeKind =
  | 'prerequisite'              // promoted, attributed, reviewed
  | 'candidate_prerequisite'    // imported from a proof term or scraped
  | 'interferes_with'           // non-interference: never introduce together
  | 'encompasses'               // layering: exercising this exercises that

export interface ConceptEdge {
  id: string
  from: string
  to: string
  kind: ConceptEdgeKind
  promotedBy?: string           // null for candidates
  rationale?: string
}

Four edge kinds rather than one, because Math Academy's principles are not all the same relation. prerequisite gates introduction (mastery learning). encompasses is what makes layering computable — exercising an advanced concept exercises what it encompasses, which is how credit propagates and why a prerequisite you just used does not come due as though untouched. interferes_with is non-interference as a selection constraint: authored, not inferred, because similarity of name is a poor proxy — sin and sinh interfere, two unrelated lemmas with similar names do not. candidate_prerequisite keeps imported structure quarantined until a human promotes it.

The verifier judge

export type Judge = 'player' | 'narrator' | 'verifier'   // third kind

export interface Verdict {
  ruling: 'correct' | 'incorrect' | 'inconclusive' | 'abandoned'
  /** How it was decided. An inconclusive ruling names what it tried. */
  method: string
  /** Populated for 'incorrect': the first step that does not follow. */
  brokeAt?: { step: number; detail: string }
  /** The domain the ruling holds on. Never dropped. */
  conditions: string[]
}

inconclusive is a first-class ruling and is never scored as wrong. It is routed to human review. Scoring it wrong would train the learner on the engine's limitations, and it is the one rule in this design most likely to be quietly violated by a later convenience.

brokeAt is what replaces the reply guarantee. The game's answer to a failed turn is a salvaged offering; practice's answer is a located error. Both refuse to leave the learner with nothing — they just disagree about what "something" is.

Scheduling, and the overlap nobody has decided yet

bza already contains a spaced-repetition implementation: lib/queries/quiz.ts has SM-2 inline (interval_days, ease_factor, repetitions, next_review_at), and lib/queries/knowledge-graph.ts calls it from updateMastery with mastered at a quiz score of 0.6.

It should not be the basis for this, for two reasons that are substantive rather than stylistic:

  • SM-2 is graph-blind. It cannot know that practising an advanced concept exercised three prerequisites, so those prerequisites come due anyway and the learner re-drills what they just used. In a domain that is mostly prerequisite structure, that is the dominant inefficiency — and encompasses above exists to fix exactly it.
  • mastered = score >= 0.6 counts any success as mastery regardless of assistance. A hint-assisted correct answer and an independent one are different observations, and collapsing them is the one thing the whole evidence design is built to prevent.

Recommendation: leave bza's quiz path alone. Book comprehension and mathematical practice are different jobs, bza's graph is per-book and per-user while a concept graph is subject-wide, and the only cost of leaving both is two schedulers that never meet. Generalising bza's to be graph-aware couples a reading feature to a mathematics curriculum for no gain.

Amendment, 2026-10-15 — the recommendation above was wrong, and the callout that followed it has been resolved.

The paragraph above argues from an architectural mismatch that does not exist. The artifacts were checked afterwards and three facts contradict it:

  • The SM-2 state is already stored on graph nodes. knowledge_nodes carries interval_days, ease_factor, repetitions, next_review_at, mastered and mastery_score as columns. There is no separate cards table. A prerequisite-aware scheduler needs no new storage.
  • knowledge_edges is already a prerequisite graph. The extractor prompt, now committed at supabase/functions/build-knowledge-graph/index.ts, says verbatim: “Edges: "from" index is PREREQUISITE for "to" index (must understand A before B)”. The table has no relationship column because prerequisite is the only kind of edge it has ever held.
  • Nothing reads those edges when scheduling. updateMastery writes one row — .eq('id', nodeId) — and never touches knowledge_edges.

So “SM-2 is graph-blind” describes a missing traversal, not a missing structure. The first bullet above overstated its own conclusion: it is an argument for adding propagation, and it was used as an argument for keeping two systems apart. A hybrid — prerequisite-aware discharge over bza's existing per-book DAG — is considerably cheaper than this report claimed.

The second bullet survives and gets sharper. Collapsing assistance is tolerable in isolated SM-2 because the error stays local to one card. Under propagation it fans out: one hint-assisted success discharges review pressure on every prerequisite it claims to have exercised. That makes the assistance distinction a precondition for propagation rather than a refinement of it — propagation first would make the scheduler worse than leaving it alone.

What does not merge. knowledge_nodes is keyed per-book and per-user, so the same concept in two books is two unrelated rows with independent review state and no identity linking them. Propagation works inside a book and structurally cannot cross one. Subject-wide identity remains the concept registry's job. The conclusion is therefore a shared mechanism over two scopes, not one system — which is close to what the paragraph above recommended, reached for a different and better reason.

Revised order of work: (1) fix the assistance collapse; (2) add backwards traversal along from_node to updateMastery, with a visited-set; (3) keep the registries scope-separate.

On step 2's visited-set, correcting this amendment's own first draft. It originally justified the visited-set defensively — “acyclicity is promised only by the prompt” — as though a cycle were a data defect the traversal has to survive. That was still the wrong framing, and briefly became a wrong migration: 64 added a trigger rejecting any edge that closed a loop, which 65 then reverted. Cycles are permitted by design, and the visited-set is therefore obligatory rather than precautionary.

Cycles are ordinary in this graph rather than corrupt. Co-requisite pairs bootstrap each other — limits and continuity, matrices and linear transformations — with no fact of the matter about which comes first. Spiral curricula are two-cycles by construction: partial understanding of A enables B, and B deepens A, which is the honest encoding when nodes are one row per concept rather than per mastery-level, as knowledge_nodes is. And the extractor is handed prose, where exposition order is not prerequisite order, so mutual references are its expected output.

This matters for the mechanism and not just for termination: mutual discharge is the correct semantics for a co-requisite pair, not a degenerate case to be guarded against. An independent success at either node is evidence about both, and refreshing both is what the pair means. The visited-set makes that terminate while still crediting each node once. Only CHECK (from_node <> to_node) was kept, because a self-edge asserts nothing a two-cycle between distinct nodes does not express better.

A deliberate non-decision: a relationship column (prerequisite | corequisite | …) would let a mutual pair be labelled instead of inferred from a cycle's presence, scoping any ordering claim to the prerequisite subset. Worth doing, but it is a vocabulary change touching the extractor prompt, the graph legend and the scheduler at once, and none of the above waits on it — an untyped cyclic graph is strictly more faithful than an untyped graph with its cycles rejected.

Timing favours doing it: concept_observations and concept_point_strength are both at 0 rows, and the book graph holds 10 nodes and 6 edges across one book with one node mastered. There is no live learner state to migrate.

Resolved, 2026-10-15. The original callout read: “knowledge_nodes and knowledge_edges are referenced by lib/queries/knowledge-graph.ts but are defined in no .sql under supabase/setup/. Either an edge function creates them or a migration is untracked.”

It was the untracked-migration case. Both tables exist in bza_public in production; no edge function creates them. The DDL has been transcribed out of information_schema/pg_catalog into supabase/setup/62_knowledge_graph_ddl.sql and verified by running it inside a transaction against production and rolling back — every statement succeeds and every index reports already-exists, which checks that it matches rather than merely compiles.

The same sweep found a second untracked artifact: build-knowledge-graph was deployed and ACTIVE with no source in the repo, recoverable only from the deployed eszip's sourcemap. It is now committed, which is what makes the edge semantics above quotable rather than inferred.

Two defects were recorded rather than silently fixed, each deserving its own migration. Both have since been dealt with, and neither landed the way it was first written down.

The missing constraints, in 64 and then 65. CHECK (from_node <> to_node) was added and kept. The cycle prevention added alongside it was wrong and was reverted — see the amendment above: cycles are ordinary in a prerequisite graph over concept-level nodes, nothing required a DAG (computeLayout is a bounded relaxation and was always cycle-tolerant), and the trigger actively broke graph builds by aborting an entire batch on one mutual pair. Termination belongs to the traversal.

The grants, in 63 — and this one was not latent. The framing above is wrong twice. It is not a two-table problem: 67 relations carried the wide ACL, because migration 57 moved them in from public with ALTER TABLE SET SCHEMA, which preserves a relation's ACL instead of re-deriving it from the destination schema's defaults — and bza_public's defaults were already correct.

More seriously, “latent rather than open” was true only of the tables. The first draft of 63 looped on relkind='r' and its assertions caught that it left views and materialized views untouched — which is where the real problem was. bza_public.monthly_usage_summary is a materialized view over api_usage, and row-level security does not apply to materialized views at all; there is no security_invoker for them. anon held the full ACL, so every user's id, request volume, token consumption, cost and last-activity time was readable by anyone holding the publishable key that ships inside the frontend bundle. Confirmed with a live unauthenticated request returning real rows across nine users. It is referenced nowhere in any project; revoked against production immediately, and that endpoint now answers 401.

The rule 63 now encodes: RLS is the gate, so any relation RLS cannot gate must not be reachable by a role a browser can assume. It enforces that for ordinary views too, refusing to grant SELECT on any view lacking security_invoker=true rather than trusting that today's two happen to have it.

What this costs

Stated plainly, because the architecture section reads more encouraging than the bill.

  • A second judge path and a second scheduler. Not large, but they are new code in a subsystem whose current virtue is that adding a game is a data edit.
  • An ontology someone has to author. The real cost. Mitigated more than expected: the math-academy-scraper project already captures Math Academy's own topic prerequisite graph — hand-authored, at the granularity their pedagogy assumes. That is a seed rather than a blank page, and aligning it against mathlib's checked statements is a much smaller job than promoting mathlib edges from nothing.
  • Two spaced-repetition implementations in one product, by choice.
  • A worker dependency if math vibe coding is wanted, since the browser has no CAS.

What it does not cost: the registries, the resolvers, the scope mechanism, the honesty guards, or the games-as-data property. Those transfer unchanged, and they are the parts that took the longest to get right.

Suggested sequencing

  1. Concept registry, read-only. The fifth registry plus a scope ontology for one subject area, seeded from the scraped Math Academy graph. No practice loop. Ship a browser for it. This is where you learn whether the ontology is authorable at all, and it is cheap to abandon.
  2. The verifier judge on one task kind. Transform-into-a-named-form, scored by the worker's equivalent operation, which already returns supported / counterexample_found / inconclusive with a domain. One kind, end to end, including the inconclusive path — because that path is the one that gets skipped.
  3. The navigation reframing. Prerequisite traversal as a game, reusing the existing game's structure. This is the part that makes it feel like bza rather than like a learning app.
  4. Scheduling. Strength per knowledge point, advancement on unaided success only, credit propagation along encompasses, mixed assembly across concepts, and interferes_with as a hard selection constraint.
  5. Surface features, in the order the table implies: Emacs keymap, then the LaTeX return leg, then RPN against the Calc record shape, then the worker call for live evaluation.

Steps 1 and 2 are the minimum that proves or kills the idea. Neither touches the turn route, the reply guarantee, or listen_turns, so neither can regress the correlation games.

Open questions

  1. Is the concept ontology authorable at scale? Step 1 answers it. The measurement is the promotion rate from candidate to promoted prerequisite: a very low rate means the formal library supplies statements but not structure, which is still useful but a much smaller claim.
  2. Where do Math Academy's graph and mathlib's disagree? They will. An MA edge is a claim about learners; a mathlib edge is a fact about proofs. A conflict usually means the formal development took a route a learner should not. Those disagreements should be preserved rather than resolved by fiat — they are the most informative data in the alignment.
  3. Does the game framing actually help learning, or just engagement? Explicitly a hypothesis. The design is positioned to answer it — provenance records whether a task arrived via a traversal or a scheduler — and presupposing the benefit is what would destroy the ability to find out.
  4. Server or client for evaluation? Worker call versus Pyodide. Leaning worker, on provenance grounds rather than performance.
